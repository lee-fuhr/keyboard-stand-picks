How to choose the right keyboard stand

Updated June 2026Independently researchedNo paid placement.

Quick answer

The best keyboard stand for you depends on your playing style, mobility needs, and whether you want a built-in music stand. For stability with a heavy keyboard, a sturdy Z- or table-style stand is ideal; for quick setup and transport, an X-stand works well, but may lack the rigidity for vigorous playing. If you need a dedicated place for sheet music, consider either an integrated music stand on a multi-tier stand or a separate, adjustable music stand that pairs with any keyboard stand.

What are the main types of keyboard stands?

The three most common designs are X-stands, Z-stands, and table-style stands. X-stands are the most affordable and fold flat for easy transport, but they can wobble side-to-side under heavy keyboards or aggressive playing. Z-stands offer a wider, more stable base and typically have multiple height adjustments. They are more rigid than X-stands but still fold for portability, though they are bulkier. Table-style stands (sometimes called &#8220;furniture-style&#8221; or &#8220;stage stands&#8221;) provide a flat, wide platform that supports the keyboard across its entire length. They are the most stable choice for weighted 88-key keyboards and often include adjustable height and optional second tiers for a laptop or second keyboard.

How do I know if a keyboard stand is stable enough?

Look for stands with a wide leg spread, rubber or non-slip feet, and cross-bracing. For heavy digital pianos or stage pianos (especially 88-key weighted models), avoid thin tubular X-stands&#8212;they tend to wobble. Instead, consider a Z-stand or a table stand with locking knobs. Always check the weight capacity rating provided by the manufacturer. If you play with force or use the stand in a live performance setting, prioritize heavy-duty models with thicker steel tubing and reinforced joints. A wobbly stand not only affects your playing but can also damage the keyboard over time.

What height range should I look for?

The ideal height puts your hands at a comfortable playing level with your forearms parallel to the floor, usually between 25 and 30 inches from the floor to the top of the keyboard. Most adjustable stands cover roughly 24 to 36 inches, but confirm the range before buying. If you sit on a standard bench, look for a stand that can go low enough (around 24–26 inches) so your elbows are at a 90-degree angle. For standing play, you&#8217;ll want the top of the keyboard near waist height, typically 32–36 inches. Some stands offer continuous height adjustment rather than preset holes, which makes fine-tuning easier.

Should I get a stand with a built-in music stand?

A built-in music stand is convenient because it attaches directly to the keyboard stand, saving floor space and keeping your sheet music at a consistent distance. However, many built-in stands are small or flimsy, only holding a few pages or a single book. If you work with full scores, thick sheet music books, or a tablet, a separate heavy-duty music stand is often more practical. Separate stands offer larger shelves, tilt adjustments, and better stability. They can be placed to the left of the keyboard or centered behind it, depending on your setup. The trade-off is that they take up extra floor space and add one more item to carry.

What about portability and folding?

If you move the stand frequently (band practice, gigs, lessons), an X-stand or folding Z-stand is the lightest and easiest to carry. Many fold flat and fit into a padded carry bag. Table-style stands are heavier and bulkier, often requiring disassembly. Consider the weight of the stand itself: a heavy-duty table stand can weigh 30 pounds or more, while an X-stand may be under 10. Also look at quick-release mechanisms that let you set up or break down in under a minute without tools. For permanent home use, portability matters less, and stability should be your top priority.

How much should I spend on a keyboard stand?

Expect to pay anywhere from budget-friendly to premium depending on build quality and features. Entry-level X-stands are very affordable and fine for light use. Mid-range Z-stands or basic table stands offer much better stability for a reasonable step up in cost. High-end table stands with multi-tier capability, tool-free adjustments, and extra accessories (like mic mounts or laptop arms) fall into the upper range. For a permanent home setup with a weighted keyboard, investing in a solid stand that won&#8217;t wobble is worth the extra money. Avoid the cheapest models if you own a keyboard over 30 pounds or plan to play with emotion.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use an X-stand for a heavy 88-key keyboard?

You can, but it&#8217;s not recommended for heavy, weighted keyboards because most X-stands lack lateral stability. A heavy keyboard makes the stand rock side-to-side during play, which can be distracting and potentially damaging. A Z-stand or table stand is safer for keyboards over 30 pounds.

Are Z-stands more stable than X-stands?

Yes, generally. The inverted &#8220;Z&#8221; shape creates a wider footprint and better weight distribution, reducing wobble. Z-stands typically have thicker tubing and more robust locking mechanisms, making them a popular choice for semi-pro and gigging musicians.

Do I need a third tier for a synth or laptop?

Only if you regularly play a second keyboard or need your laptop or tablet at eye level. Many table stands and some Z-stands offer optional second or third tiers. Before buying, measure the depth you need&#8212;tiers are usually adjustable but may not fit extra-wide gear.

What is the ideal height for a keyboard stand?

The ideal height allows your elbows to form roughly a 90-degree angle when your hands rest on the keyboard. For seated play, that typically translates to a keyboard height of 26 to 30 inches from the floor. For standing, aim for 34 to 37 inches. Always measure your bench or standing posture to match.

How do I set up a keyboard stand properly?

First, read the manufacturer&#8217;s instructions and ensure all locking knobs or pins are fully engaged. Place the stand on a level floor and test it for wobble before setting your keyboard on it. Make sure the stand&#8217;s arms or straps support the keyboard at its outer thirds, not just the center. Tighten everything firmly, and recheck after a few hours of play.

Can I attach a music stand to any keyboard stand?

Not all keyboard stands have a dedicated mount for a music stand. Some models include a built-in clip or rod attachment, but most require a separate freestanding music stand. If you want an integrated solution, look for stands advertised with an included music stand or a universal adapter option.

In shortYour keyboard stand is the foundation of your playing setup. Prioritize stability over novelty, match the height to your playing position, and decide early whether you need a built-in music stand or prefer a separate one. The right stand will make your practice or performance more comfortable and reliable for years to come.
